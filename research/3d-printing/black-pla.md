# Comparing black PLA

Data: [`lists/filament.yaml`](../../lists/filament.yaml) → item `pla-black`.

Recorded: 2026-11-27 from Josh's prints and earlier online research; product stock and prices were not refreshed during this import.

## Need

Josh's Kingroon HS black prints well but looks charcoal/dark gray beside some molded black objects. He wants to compare black levels, not discard functional filament. A real photo of the organizer beside other objects confirmed that the difference is visible beyond the printer's chamber-camera exposure.

## Options summary

Ordinary Sunlu black PLA and Elegoo black PLA were selected as small comparison purchases. Both appeared in an Amazon cart; purchase completion is unconfirmed. The historical cart prices live in YAML. Sunlu “Midnight Black” should not be assumed to be a neutral black: earlier research found that name associated with a navy-looking shade.

Drying can improve printing and surface defects, but does not intrinsically change a pigment into a deeper black. Temperature, extrusion speed, surface texture, gloss, illumination, and camera exposure all affect perceived darkness. A matte or textured surface can look lighter than a smooth surface made from similarly dark plastic.

## Recommendation

**Buy:** Compare one spool each of the selected Sunlu and Elegoo standard black before committing to another large black pack.

**Runner-up:** Consider a premium black such as [Atomic Extreme Jet Black](https://atomicfilament.com/products/extreme-jet-black-pla-filament) only if the sample comparison leaves a meaningful gap. Product marketing is not a guarantee of a shade match.

**Why:** Black appearance is product-, finish-, and batch-specific. A supplier tier or the words PLA+, HS, or matte cannot establish how dark a particular print will look.

## A useful controlled comparison

1. Use the same small model with a flat top and a vertical wall for Kingroon, Sunlu, and Elegoo. Put a label underneath each sample.
2. Keep nozzle, layer height, orientation, and comparable safe print settings consistent. Record any temperature/profile differences needed for each material rather than hiding them.
3. Compare the samples together under the same light and viewing angle, away from the bright chamber lamp. Photograph them together if retaining a record.
4. If a finish changes markedly with speed or temperature, print a small second sample to distinguish the finish effect from the pigment difference.
